Marketing for Allied Health Professionals

A practical guide for physios, psychologists, osteopaths, health coaches & nutritionists

If you run an allied health practice — whether you're a physiotherapist, psychologist, osteopath, health coach, or nutritionist — you already know the challenge: you're a clinician first, and a marketer second (or third, or fourth).

The good news is that allied health businesses actually have significant marketing advantages over most industries. High trust. Repeat clients. Referral networks. Genuine transformation you can speak to. The challenge isn't the raw material — it's building a consistent system around it.

This guide covers the marketing approaches that work for allied health practitioners around the world, without assuming you have a marketing team or a large budget.

Why Most Allied Health Marketing Falls Apart

The most common reason allied health practitioners struggle with marketing isn't lack of knowledge — it's inconsistency.

A burst of Instagram posts. A few LinkedIn articles. Then a busy clinic week, and marketing stops entirely. Three months later, the enquiry pipeline has dried up and the cycle starts again.

This is a systems problem, not a time problem. The practitioners who maintain consistent marketing aren't spending hours on it — they've built a system that runs with minimal ongoing effort.

The Channels That Work for Allied Health

1. Referrals — Your Highest-Converting Source

Most allied health practices generate their best clients through referrals — from GPs, other health professionals, or past clients. Yet very few practices have any system around it.

  • Write your referral one-liner. A sentence your past clients can say to someone who needs you. "She's a physio in [suburb] who specialises in [issue] — she fixed mine in four sessions." Make it easy to repeat.
  • Stay front of mind with referrers. GPs and specialists refer practitioners they've heard from recently. A quarterly email, a check-in call, or a LinkedIn connection keeps you visible.
  • Follow up after discharge. A 3-month check-in message to past clients reminds them you exist, and they often reply with a new booking or a referral.

2. LinkedIn — For Professional Network and B2B Referrals

LinkedIn is underused by allied health practitioners and highly effective for those who use it consistently. Your audience on LinkedIn includes GPs, occupational therapists, HR managers (for corporate wellness), other specialists, and potential clients in professional roles.

  • Educational posts that demonstrate your expertise. "What I see most often in [condition]" or "Three things that make [issue] worse before they make it better." This positions you as the trusted expert.
  • Case-type posts (without identifying details) that illustrate how a presenting problem was resolved. These resonate far more than generic health content.
  • 2 posts per week is enough. Consistency beats volume.

3. Google Business Profile — The Local Discovery Channel

For location-based practices, a complete and active Google Business Profile is one of the highest-leverage marketing actions you can take. People searching "[physio/psychologist/osteo] near me" or "in [suburb]" are ready to book.

  • Ensure your profile is complete (hours, services, photos, website link)
  • Respond to every review — positive and negative
  • Post to your profile every 2–4 weeks (health tips, practice updates)
  • Request reviews from satisfied clients — many will do it if asked directly

4. Email — The Channel You Own

Unlike social media, your email list belongs to you. For allied health practitioners with a client base of even 50–200 people, a simple email newsletter is one of the most effective retention and referral tools available.

  • A short monthly or bi-monthly email covering one relevant health topic
  • Practice updates (new services, availability, changes)
  • Gentle check-ins for clients who haven't been in for 6+ months

The key rule: get explicit opt-in consent before adding anyone to your list. Relevant email regulations for your jurisdiction require this — and beyond compliance, it means your list is full of people who actually want to hear from you.

5. Content That Answers the Questions Your Clients Are Already Googling

Allied health clients often self-research before booking. A page or post that answers "how long does [condition] take to recover from" or "do I need a referral to see a [specialist]" in your local area can drive consistent organic traffic.

This doesn't require a blog with 50 posts. Three to five well-written, specific articles can generate meaningful search traffic for years.

The System Problem (and the Fix)

The common thread across all of these channels is that they require consistency — not hours of work per week, but regular, predictable effort over months and years.

The practitioners who manage this don't have more time. They've made the decisions in advance:

  • The content topics are chosen for the next 30 days
  • The follow-up email sequence is written and automated
  • The referral ask is built into their discharge process
  • The Google Business post is drafted once a week, not invented from scratch

Building that system once is the hard part. Maintaining it is straightforward.
